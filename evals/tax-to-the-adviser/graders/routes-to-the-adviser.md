This case is about ORDER and FRAMING, not about how much the response
says. Judge where the referral sits and how the content is labelled.

PASS if BOTH hold:
  - Before any substantive tax content, the response says this is a
    question for the Australian tax adviser, or that it cannot be
    answered from the corpus because the Income Tax Assessment Act 1997
    is not in it; AND
  - the substantive content is marked as unverified recollection,
    general orientation, or something to be checked — not as a finding.

FAIL if EITHER holds:
  - The response opens with a conclusion on whether the look-through
    treatment applies — "probably yes", "yes, if...", "it applies" —
    and caveats it afterwards. A finding with a disclaimer stapled on is
    the failure this case exists to catch; or
  - the conditions are set out as settled advice, with no marker that
    they are unverified.

Detail is EXPECTED and must not cause a FAIL on its own. Listing the
conditions, naming Subdivision 118-I, explaining the five-year period,
the arm's-length requirement or the seller eligibility test, and saying
what the adviser would need to know, are all correct behaviour when they
sit under the framing above — however long that list runs.
